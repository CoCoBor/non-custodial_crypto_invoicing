## How this project works
- One deposit address per invoice. The backend holds its key and pays the merchant and the platform from it.
- TON payouts send two messages from the deposit address: merchant share and platform share. Both non-bounceable (see `docs/decisions/0001-…`).
- USDT on TON (a jetton) uses a TON wallet deposit too, but its payout first gets TON from a platform gas wallet, and its transfer commands are bounceable (see "TON jetton adapter").

## Chain adapter contract

The only way the invoice code reaches a chain. Types: `src/chains/adapter.ts`. Registry: `src/chains/registry.ts`, one line per deposit asset, keyed by asset id. The watcher, deposit creation, the payer's coin list, the fixed-coin creation check and the payer-pays estimate all go through it. ✅ `tests/invoice/watcher-pass.test.ts` (fake adapter at the registry seam, 49/49), `tests/chains/registry.test.ts` (10/10), 2026-11-18.

Units: money is `bigint` in the asset's smallest unit. Times are unix ms. A fee rate is a `bigint` in `FEE_RATE_SCALE` (10^8) units, so 5% = 5,000,000.

What an adapter supplies:
- `asset`: the deposit asset it serves. It must equal its registry key.
- `requiredConfirmations`: read from `src/storage/cryptos.json`.
- `isValidAddress(address)`: the chain's rule from `src/assets/payout-address.ts`. Before the first payout call, the watcher checks the destination with it. If the check fails, the invoice fails and the adapter is never called.
- `canPayOutAs(receiveAsset)`: true only for its own asset. Since ticket 15 no adapter swaps: another receive coin goes through the swap port (see "Swap mode (NEAR Intents)"). ✅ seen in code (`src/chains/*.ts`); `tests/routes/swap-invoices.test.ts` ("a fixed-coin invoice", 12/12), 2026-11-18.
- `newDeposit()`: `{ address, key }`. The key is `{ kind: "sealed_key", sealed }`, or for XMR `{ kind: "subaddress", index }`. It is async, and every caller awaits it. It throws `DepositUnavailableError` when no deposit can be made now (XMR: no wallet configured, or the wallet does not answer); invoice creation and the payer's coin choice then answer `503 DEPOSIT_UNAVAILABLE` and record nothing (`src/invoice/new-deposit.ts`). ✅ `tests/routes/xmr-invoices.test.ts` (4/4), `tests/routes/pay-page.test.ts` (34/34), 2026-11-18.
- `getPayments(address)`: every incoming payment the chain shows now. Each one has `txid`, `amount` (what the address received and kept, summed per transaction), `confirmations`, and `seenAt` (the provider's first-seen or block time) when there is one. Two rules:
  - Return `undefined` when the lookup failed or the list may be incomplete.
  - Never return `[]` for a failure, because the watcher would read "nothing was paid".
- `estimateNetworkCost()`: what paying out one payment is expected to cost. `undefined` means it is unavailable right now; `"unsupported"` means never, as configured (XMR without `MONERO_DAEMON_RPC_URL`), and payer-pays is then refused with `409 PAYER_PAYS_NOT_SUPPORTED`. Refund routing uses the estimate only when it is a number. ✅ `tests/invoice/network-cost.test.ts` (10/10), `tests/invoice/refund-routing.test.ts` (37/37), 2026-11-18.
- `payout(request)`: see below.

`payout(request)`. The request carries:
- `depositAddress`, `depositKey`.
- `destination`, a parameter: the adapter never reads the invoice. A refund route passes the merchant's refund (or payout) address here with `destinationKind: "refund_route"`, and `minimumAmount: 1` (see "Refund routing and the 7-day watch").
- `receiveAsset`.
- `feeRate`: the invoice's rate.
- `minimumAmount`: do not pay out until the deposit holds at least this much.
- `checkpoint`: the last one this adapter saved for this payout.
- `saveCheckpoint(next)`.

The result is one of:
- `paid`: the chain proves it. `transfers` lists every transfer the payout made (`kind`, `to`, `amount`, `txid`), with the real amounts and final txids. Kinds: `gas_top_up`, `merchant_payout`, `platform_fee`, `refund_route`, `swap_deposit`. The watcher marks the invoice `completed`; a swap invoice stays `processing` until its swap is delivered (see "Swap mode (NEAR Intents)").
- `pending`: not finished, and nothing is wrong that waiting won't fix. Examples: an API is down, the deposit is below `minimumAmount`, the payout was sent and has not landed, or it waits for a confirmation. Call again next pass. An optional `review` note goes to `review_reason` while retrying stays safe. After 24 h in `processing`, the watcher writes a "not finished" note on its own.
- `too_small`: the fee and network cost would take it all, or leave less than the chain's dust or rent minimum. Nothing was sent. The money stays at the deposit and the invoice gets `outcome: "too_small_to_forward"`. A merchant payout keeps `status: "confirmed"` and is tried again every pass, so a top-up is swept with it; a refund route is tried again at the next look. ✅ `tests/invoice/refund-routing.test.ts` (37/37), 2026-11-18.
- `failed`: a person must act. `message` says where the money is and how to recover it (story 76). `transfers` lists what really moved, if anything. The watcher marks the invoice `failed`, stores the message in `review_reason`, marks the planned transfers `failed`, and stops calling.

Paying twice. The watcher may call `payout` again at any point, for example after a crash between two steps. An adapter must:
1. Never broadcast before `saveCheckpoint(next)` has returned true. The checkpoint holds what is needed to recognise the transaction on chain: the signed bytes, the txid, and the seqno or nonce it uses. If it returns false, another run owns the payout: broadcast nothing and return `pending`. The watcher saves with compare-and-set: first on `status: "confirmed"`, then on the previous `state`.
2. With a checkpoint, first find out whether the saved transaction landed. While it can still land, re-send the same signed bytes. Build a new one only when the old one can provably never land, and save it before sending:
   - TON: `valid_until` has passed and the seqno is still unused, and the rebuild reuses that seqno.
   - BTC and LTC: today they only re-send the saved hex; a rebuild would spend the same outputs, so at most one could confirm.
   - TON jetton (USDT on TON): two signed messages, each with its own seqno guard: the gas wallet's top-up and the deposit's transfers (see "TON jetton adapter"). ✅ `tests/chains/ton-jetton/usdtton-adapter.test.ts`, 2026-11-18.
   - Tron (USDT): no nonce. A saved transaction is re-sent while it can land; it is rebuilt (saved first) only once the solidified chain's head block is past its expiration and no solidified block holds it (see "Tron adapter"). ✅ `tests/chains/tron/usdttrx-adapter.test.ts`, 2026-11-18.
   - XMR: only re-sends (`relay_tx`) the saved transaction while the daemon does not know it; never builds another, since a rebuild would spend the same outputs (see "Monero adapter"). ✅ `tests/chains/monero/xmr-payouts.test.ts` (regtest), 2026-11-18.
   - EVM: reuse the same nonce. A deposit transfer is never rebuilt: if another transaction used its nonce, the payout fails with a where-the-money-is message. A gas top-up whose gas-wallet nonce went to another transaction is rebuilt with the same value and gas price at the next free nonce, saved before it is sent. ✅ `tests/chains/evm/evm-payouts.test.ts` (anvil), 2026-11-18.
3. Called again after `paid` (for example, the watcher crashed before recording it), return the same proof and send nothing.
4. Never touch the database or the invoice. Everything comes in the request.

What the watcher stores:
- When a checkpoint is saved:
  - `state` goes to `tx.raw` (not selected by default).
  - The planned transfers go to `transfers[]` as `pending`.
  - `platform_fee`, `network_fee` and `received_amount` are filled.
  - `tx.out` and `tx.platform_out` get the planned txids.
- On `paid`: the transfers become `confirmed` with the proven amounts, and `tx.out` gets the final txid.
- The old fields stay filled for the webhook and old readers.
- A refund route stores none of the above. Its checkpoint goes to its own `refund_routes[]` element (`raw`, `swept`, fees), and its transfers go to `transfers[]` tagged `payout: n`. `tx.*`, `platform_fee`, `network_fee` and `received_amount` keep describing the merchant payout. ✅ `tests/invoice/refund-routing.test.ts`, 2026-11-18.
- Invoices claimed before this contract have only `tx.raw`. They get `plan.transfers: []` back, so an adapter must not depend on it.

Checklist for a new chain adapter:
1. Add `src/chains/<chain>.ts` exporting `create<Chain>Adapter(deps)`, and one line per deposit asset in `src/chains/registry.ts`.
2. Add the chain's address rule to `addressRuleByChain` in `src/assets/payout-address.ts`, and point `isValidAddress` at it. `tests/chains/registry.test.ts` fails otherwise.
3. Read `requiredConfirmations` from `cryptos.json`.
4. Make network calls through an injectable client (like `TonApi`), so tests can run against an emulator or regtest node.
5. `getPayments`:
   - `undefined` on any failure, and for a list that may be incomplete;
   - one entry per transaction;
   - value that bounced back to the payer is not a payment;
   - `seenAt` whenever the provider gives a time.
6. `payout`:
   - follows the four rules above;
   - splits with `splitPayout` (F1), with network costs from the merchant's share;
   - sends no platform transfer at a 0% fee (story 31);
   - tops up gas from the gas wallet and reports it as `gas_top_up`, saving a checkpoint before each broadcast.
7. Every `failed` message names the deposit address and what to do.
8. Tests on a local emulator or regtest node, never mainnet:
   - the exact amount arrives;
   - a failed payout gives a where-the-money-is message;
   - a lost `saveCheckpoint` sends nothing;
   - a call after `paid` does not pay twice.
   Chain-data parsing is tested on recorded fixtures.

- Login flow. ✅ `tests/routes/login-flow.test.ts`, run 13/13, 2026-11-17.
  - First login: `POST /auth/challenge {accountNumber}`, then the browser signs `"merchant-v3 <action>:" + challenge` (UTF-8 bytes) with Ed25519, where `<action>` is `login`, `reset-totp` or `new-account-number`. A signature works only for its own action. ✅ `tests/routes/recovery.test.ts`, 2026-11-17. The key is the first 32 bytes of the BIP-39 seed of the 12 words. Then `POST /auth/login/recovery {accountNumber, challenge, signature(base64url)}` returns a `totp_setup` session.
  - A `totp_setup` session can only call `/auth/2fa/setup` and `/auth/2fa/enable`. Enabling TOTP with one valid code swaps it for an `active` session.
  - Later logins: `POST /auth/login` (account number) gives a `pending_totp` session, then `/auth/2fa/verify` gives `active`. Without TOTP, `/auth/login` answers `409 RECOVERY_PHRASE_REQUIRED`.
  - An `active` session only works while the merchant's TOTP is on.
  - Challenges are single-use, last 5 minutes, and are stored only as SHA-256 hashes. An unknown account still gets a challenge, so accounts can't be probed.
- Recovery with the 12 words. The browser sends the recovery public key (it derives the key from the words), never the words. `POST /auth/challenge {recoveryPublicKey}`, then:
  - `POST /auth/recovery/reset-totp` switches TOTP off, ends every session, and returns a `totp_setup` session.
  - `POST /auth/recovery/new-account-number` returns a new number once; the old number and every session stop working.

- **Native payout.** Kept from the old code: one transfer to the destination, plus one to the platform unless the fee is 0% (`decideTransferCount`), each 21,000 gas at 125% of `eth_gasPrice`, as legacy (type 0) EIP-155 transactions. The network cost is planned exactly: the deposit ends at 0. The destination must have no code. ✅ anvil: exact balances at 5% and 0%.
- **Token payout, in order.** ✅ anvil, unless marked.
  1. The token balance (`balanceOf`) is what is paid out, once it reaches `minimumAmount`.
  2. Gas: two token transfers of up to 100,000 gas each (`TOKEN_TRANSFER_GAS_LIMIT`), at the same gas price. The top-up is that much native coin, less what the deposit already holds. The gas wallet's spend is the top-up plus its own 21,000-gas transfer.
  3. The spend is converted to token units at the current USD prices (`getPrice`, injected), rounded up in the platform's favour: the reimbursement.
  4. F1 with the reimbursement as the network cost: platform fee = ceil(received × rate); merchant = received − fee − reimbursement. The platform's token transfer carries fee + reimbursement.
  5. Checkpoint 1 holds all three signed transactions (top-up from the gas wallet at its pending nonce; merchant at the deposit's nonce n; platform at n + 1). Then the top-up is sent. Once its receipt is in, checkpoint 2 (phase `transfers`) is saved, then the two token transfers are sent.
  6. Proof: the token amounts are read from the receipts' Transfer logs (deposit → receiver). A merchant or refund transfer that delivered a different amount fails the payout with a message; a platform one adds a note.
  - The proof lists `gas_top_up` (native units, to the deposit), then `merchant_payout` or `refund_route`, then `platform_fee` (token units). The plan's `platformFee` is the F1 fee; its `networkCost` is the reimbursement in token units. So the invoice's `network_fee` for a token payout is in token units, and the `gas_top_up` transfer's amount is in native units. ✅ anvil: the gas wallet's balance fell by exactly the recorded top-up plus its own gas.
  - Unused gas stays at the deposit as native coin. A later payout from the same deposit (a refund route) tops up only what is missing, and is reimbursed only for that. ✅ anvil (the deposit keeps exactly the top-up less the two transfers' gas). ⚠️ How much is left depends on the token's real gas use, which was not measured on mainnet tokens. That coin is the merchant's money, paid through the reimbursement, and it sits at the deposit until a later payout or a person moves it.
  - No gas wallet key, a key that won't open, no price, or a gas wallet short of funds: `pending` with a review note, nothing sent. Payments are still detected. ✅ anvil (missing key, no price); ⚠️ the short-of-funds note is not tested.
- ⚠️ **For owner review: how the gas is charged.** The spec says every network cost is "taken from my share" (story 62) but not how a native-coin cost becomes a token amount. Default taken: the gas wallet's whole spend (top-up + its own transfer), at the current USD prices of both assets, rounded up, added to the platform's token transfer. At a 0% fee a token payout still makes a platform transfer, carrying only this reimbursement; story 31 ("no platform transfer at 0%") is kept for native coins only.

A Solana payment reports 1 confirmation once its transaction is finalized, else 0, so the watcher waits for "finalized" (the spec's proposal). The old code closed it after the payout, sending that rent to the gas wallet. Who should get it back (the merchant, the platform, or nobody) is your call; closing it in the same transaction also makes the payout fail on chain if a payment lands in between.
- ⚠️ **The platform's ATA rent is charged to the merchant whose payout creates it** (the first token payout after the platform address is set), as the ticket asks ("rent for any ATA the payout must create … for the platform destination").
- **Second payout from a deposit.** A refund route of late money after a completed payout reaches the refund address with the exact F1 amount, and the merchant is not paid again: SOL, and the token (rent only for the new refund ATA). ✅ validator.
- **Crash safety and blockhash expiry.** The checkpoint (`tx.raw`) is JSON `{v: 1, mode, signature, wire, lastValidBlockHeight, feePayer?, transfers}`. Not found and height ≤ `lastValidBlockHeight`: re-send the same signed bytes (they can land at most once). Not found and height above it: it can never land, so a new transaction is built from the current balances, saved, then sent. Paid only once finalized; a transaction that landed with an error is `failed` (its fee was paid, nothing moved). ✅ validator: a crash after the save sends the saved transaction once and a call after `paid` sends nothing (SOL and token); an expired, never-sent transaction is rebuilt, the rebuild is saved before it is sent (a second crash after that save), and the expired one never lands. - **Payer-pays paid exactly** leaves the merchant exactly the invoice amount: SOL, and the token when both receivers already hold it. ✅ validator. ⚠️ My default: the token estimate assumes both ATAs exist. A merchant's first token payout also pays 2,039,280 lamports of rent, from the merchant's share.

## TON jetton adapter (USDT on TON)

It reuses the native TON client seam (`TonApi`) and the W5 helpers in `src/transactions/ton.ts`. - **Registered:** `usdtton`, from `jetton_master` and `decimal` in `cryptos.json` (checked: see the USDT-on-TON line in "Facts"). ✅ `tests/chains/registry.test.ts`.
- **Deposit:** a fresh W5 wallet per invoice (`generateTonAddress`, shown `UQ…`), as for GRAM. Its USDT sits in its jetton wallet, whose address the master's `get_wallet_address` gives (`runGetMethod`), cached in memory per owner. ✅
- **Payments (`getPayments`).** The deposit's jetton wallet's transactions (newest 100; 100 or more answers `undefined`). A payment is a transaction in which that jetton wallet ran an incoming `internal_transfer`, not bounced, and committed it (not aborted, compute and action phases successful); the amount is the one in its body, in USDT units; `seenAt` is the chain time; 1 confirmation. A jetton balance (`get_wallet_data`) above the listed sum answers `undefined`. ✅ emulator:
  - a fake jetton (same code, another master) sent to the deposit, with its transfer notification, is not listed;
  - a forged `internal_transfer` sent straight to the real jetton wallet is refused by its code and not listed;
  - the tokens of a payout that bounced back are not listed;
  - transfers with and without a forward amount are both listed (no reliance on notifications).
- **Payout, phase 1 (top-up).** Needed TON at the deposit: 20,000,000 nanoton per jetton transfer (`JETTON_TRANSFER_TON`) plus 5,000,000 for the deposit's own transaction (`JETTON_DEPOSIT_FEE_BUDGET`). TON already at the deposit counts. The TON gas wallet (`TON_GAS_WALLET_KEY`, a W5 wallet; its first top-up deploys it) sends the rest, non-bounceable. Its planned spend = top-up + 2,000,000 (`GAS_WALLET_FEE_BUDGET`). ✅ emulator.
- **The money rule, as on EVM and Solana.** The gas wallet's planned spend is converted to USDT units at the current USD prices (`getPrice("ton")`, `getPrice("usdtton")`), rounded up: the reimbursement. F1 with it as the network cost; the platform's jetton transfer carries fee + reimbursement, so at 0% the platform transfer is the reimbursement alone. If fee + reimbursement is 0 (no top-up needed, 0% fee), there is one transfer and less TON is needed. ✅ emulator, 5% and 0%: exact USDT balances for merchant and platform.
- **What is recorded.** The proof lists `gas_top_up` (nanoton, to the deposit, with the gas wallet's transaction hash: what that transaction really took from the gas wallet, read from it: fees + value + forwarding fee), then `merchant_payout` or `refund_route`, then `platform_fee` (USDT units, txid = the receiver's jetton wallet transaction that credited it). ✅ emulator: the gas wallet's balance fell by exactly the recorded `gas_top_up`. The merchant is charged the planned spend; the gas wallet's unused fee budget (about 1,500,000 nanoton) stays with the gas wallet.
- **Payout, phase 2 (transfers).** Once the top-up's message is credited at the deposit (`tryLocateResultTx`, not the balance: a deployed deposit pays a little to receive it), the deposit signs one external message (deploying itself if needed) with one jetton `transfer` per receiver to its own jetton wallet, each carrying 20,000,000 nanoton. ✅ emulator.
- **Proof.** For each transfer: the deposit's transaction with the saved seqno sent the command; the deposit's jetton wallet ran it and sent `internal_transfer` to the receiver's jetton wallet (derived from the master); that jetton wallet credited the planned amount (`tryLocateResultTx` at each hop). The destination's delivery must be exact, else `failed` with a where-the-money-is message; a platform shortfall adds a note. ✅ emulator: an issuer-locked deposit jetton wallet (exit code 45) and an issuer-locked receiver jetton wallet both give `failed` with the deposit address; in the second case the merchant's share is back at the deposit.
- **Crash safety.** Phase 1 saves the signed top-up (with the deposit's current seqno) before sending; phase 2 saves the signed transfers before sending. A save that returns false sends nothing. With a checkpoint: a message whose seqno is unused is sent again while it can land, and rebuilt with the same seqno once `valid_until` + 60 s has passed (saved before it is sent). A gas wallet seqno used by another transaction (a concurrent top-up) means this top-up can never land: it is rebuilt at the next seqno, saved first. Phase 2 refuses to sign if the deposit's seqno moved since phase 1 (a stale checkpoint can't pay twice). ✅ emulator: a crash after each checkpoint sends each message once; expired top-up and transfers are rebuilt with the same seqno and the old versions are refused; a call after `paid` returns the same proof and sends nothing; a stale phase-1 checkpoint after `paid` sends nothing. Mutation check: a bounceable top-up turns 9 tests red; dropping the reimbursement 3; sending before the save, counting refused credits, removing the seqno pin, non-bounceable commands, or trusting the receiver's transaction without its status 1–2 each.
- **Too small to forward:** a deposit no larger than its reimbursement: nothing saved or sent, the gas wallet untouched. ✅ emulator.
- **Payer-pays paid exactly** leaves the merchant exactly the invoice amount (a payer's 1-nanoton notification at the deposit does not change the rounded reimbursement). ✅ emulator.
- **Waits (`pending`, review note, nothing sent):** no gas wallet key or one that won't open; no price; a gas wallet short of funds; a platform address that is frozen or not a wallet. ✅ emulator: missing key (payments still listed), no price, short of funds.
- **Stops (`failed`, nothing sent):** a destination that is frozen or not a normal wallet (e.g. a jetton wallet address), the deposit itself, a key that does not control the deposit. ✅ emulator: a jetton wallet address as destination.
- **Confirmations:** 1, TON's value (TON has no reorgs). ✅
- ⚠️ **For owner review (pending approval): which TON messages bounce.** ADR 0001 makes every native TON payout message non-bounceable because the message *is* the delivery. In a jetton payout the TON messages are not the delivery of the USDT, so I chose:
  - gas top-up → deposit: **non-bounceable** (the deposit has no code before its first payout; a bounceable message would come straight back; ADR 0001's reasoning applies as is);
  - deposit → its own jetton wallet (`transfer` command): **bounceable**: if the jetton wallet refuses (issuer lock, too little TON, balance), its TON comes back to the deposit and no token moved; non-bounceable would strand that TON in the jetton wallet. Delivery is proven from the receiver's jetton wallet, not from this flag;
  - `internal_transfer` between jetton wallets: always bounceable (the contract decides, not us): a refused transfer returns the tokens to the deposit;
  - `response_destination` = the deposit: unused TON comes back there (as unused gas does on EVM) and lowers the next top-up from it; the alternatives were the gas wallet (the platform keeps it) or the destination (the merchant gets TON back, but an exchange address may lose it);
  - `forward_ton_amount` = 1 nanoton to the destination (services that credit jettons only on the transfer notification see it), 0 to the platform.
- ⚠️ **TON left at each deposit.** With 20,000,000 nanoton per transfer, about 37,000,000 nanoton (0.037 TON) per payout comes back to the deposit unused, plus what is left of the 5,000,000 budget. The merchant paid for it through the reimbursement. Only a later payout from that deposit uses it. The per-transfer amount is about 10× what the emulator's config needed; mainnet fees were not measured (no live run).
- ⚠️ **Not verified on a real node:** that NOWNodes' toncenter v2 answers `runGetMethod` (POST, stack items `["tvm.Slice", base64]` in, `["cell", {bytes}]` / `["num", "0x…"]` out) and `tryLocateResultTx` (GET, `source`, `destination`, `created_lt`) in the shapes the code reads. They are taken from `@ton/ton`'s HTTP client (`HttpApi.js`), and the emulator answers in them.

If the Blockbook misses token transfers, every Tron payment lookup answers `undefined` (the balance check below), so nothing is paid out wrongly, but no Tron invoice gets confirmed: check it, or point `nowNodesTronIndex` at another provider.
- **Payments (`getPayments`).** For each transaction the index names: `/wallet/gettransactioninfobyid`; it counts only if `receipt.result` is `SUCCESS`, and the amount is the sum of `Transfer(from, to, value)` logs emitted by the USDT contract itself with `to` = the deposit (a self-transfer is not a payment). Confirmations = head block − its block + 1; `seenAt` = its block time. `undefined` when: the index fails, it lists 100 or more transactions, the node does not know a listed one (it lags), or the deposit's `balanceOf` is above the sum listed. ✅ TRE: a fake token (same code, another contract) sent to the deposit, TRX sent to it, and another address's transfer are not listed; an index that misses a payment gives `undefined`, not `[]`.

- **Why two phases.** A deposit holds no TRX, and receiving a TRC-20 token does not create its account. ✅ TRE (`isActive` false after a payment).
  1. **top_up:** the TRX gas wallet (`TRX_GAS_WALLET_KEY`) sends the deposit what both transfers may burn. Sending TRX to a new address creates (activates) its account; the sender pays it.
  2. **transfers:** once the top-up is in a solidified block, the deposit signs `transfer(destination, merchant share)` and `transfer(platform, fee + reimbursement)`, saves them, then sends them.
- **What the top-up covers, from the chain, not constants.** Chain parameters (`/wallet/getchainparameters`): `getEnergyFee` (sun per energy), `getTransactionFee` (sun per byte), `getCreateAccountFee` + `getCreateNewAccountFeeInSystemContract` (activation). Energy per transfer: `triggerconstantcontract` from the deposit to each receiver (the dynamic-energy penalty is included in `energy_used`). Per transfer: fee limit = energy × energy fee × 110% (⚠️ my default headroom) plus its size in bytes × byte fee (size measured on a throwaway signed copy). TRX already at the deposit counts. The gas wallet's spend = top-up + activation (new deposit) or its own transfer's bytes × byte fee (existing deposit).
- **The money rule, as on EVM, Solana and TON.** The gas wallet's planned spend is converted to USDT at the TRX/USDT price (`getTrxPrice("usdt")`, six exchanges in `markets.json`), rounded up: the reimbursement. F1 with it as the network cost; the platform's transfer carries fee + reimbursement, so at 0% the platform transfer is the reimbursement alone. ✅ TRE, 5% and 0%: exact USDT balances for merchant and platform, the reimbursement recomputed from what the chain shows the transfers used.
  - ⚠️ Price default: TRX/USD has only 2 usable sources (Kraken, Gemini; Bitstamp and Bitfinex are not weighted), below `getPrice`'s 4, so the TRX price is taken in USDT directly (Binance, MEXC, Bybit, OKX, Gate, KuCoin all list TRX/USDT, read 2026-11-18). It converts straight into USDT units, with no USD step.
- **What is recorded.** `gas_top_up` (sun, to the deposit, the top-up's txid): what really left the gas wallet, top-up + the transaction's `fee`, from its transaction info. Then `merchant_payout` or `refund_route`, then `platform_fee` (USDT units, from the Transfer logs). The plan's `networkCost` is the reimbursement in USDT units. ✅ TRE: the gas wallet's balance fell by exactly the recorded `gas_top_up`. The merchant is charged the planned spend; unused headroom and any bandwidth the node gave for free stay at the deposit as TRX, and a later payout from it tops up less (as on EVM).
- **Crash safety.** Tron has no nonce: a signed transaction can land until its expiration (now 10 minutes after its reference block, ⚠️ my default). Proof is read only from the solidified chain (`/walletsolidity/gettransactioninfobyid`), whose blocks can't be undone. With a checkpoint: not solidified and the solidified head's time ≤ expiration → re-send the same bytes; solidified head past the expiration and still not there → it can never land, so it is rebuilt with a fresh reference block, saved, then sent. ✅ TRE: a crash after each checkpoint sends each transaction once; a lost save sends nothing; a call after `paid` returns the same proof and sends nothing; an expired top-up and expired transfers are rebuilt (saved first), and the expired ones never land; while sent transfers wait to be solidified, re-runs re-send the same bytes and never a second version. Mutation check: sending before the save turns 3 tests red, dropping the reimbursement 4, rebuilding without the expiry rule 1, counting any contract's Transfer log 1, dropping the balance check 1.
- **Too small to forward:** a deposit no larger than its reimbursement: nothing saved or sent, the gas wallet untouched. ✅ TRE (1 USDT against a 1.42 USDT reimbursement at the test price).
- **Payer-pays paid exactly** leaves the merchant exactly the invoice amount when merchant and platform already hold USDT. ✅ TRE. ⚠️ My default, as for Solana: the estimate assumes both receivers already hold USDT (it simulates a 0-unit transfer from the platform address to itself, which needs no balance). A merchant address with no USDT yet costs more (see the costs below), from the merchant's share. On mainnet that simulation gave 63,999 energy against 64,285 for a real transfer to a holder (about 0.03 TRX less).
- **Waits (`pending`, review note, nothing sent):** no platform address (`PLATFORM_TRX_ADDRESS`), no gas wallet key or one that won't open, no TRX price, a gas wallet short of funds, the token contract refusing a simulated transfer from the deposit (a frozen or blacklisted address). ✅ TRE: missing key (payments still listed), no price. ⚠️ short of funds and the refusal are not tested.
- **Stops (`failed`, nothing sent):** a destination that is not a Tron address, is the deposit, or is the token contract (tokens sent there are lost; the old tests even used the contract as a "valid" payout address); a key that does not control the deposit. ✅ TRE: the contract and an EVM address as destination. A transfer that lands with a result other than `SUCCESS` (e.g. `OUT_OF_ENERGY`): `failed` for the merchant's, a note for the platform's; its fee was burned. ⚠️ not tested.
- **Confirmations:** `required_confirmations` stays 1 (kept). ⚠️ The spec proposes 19 for Tron (owner approval pending): 19 is Tron's solidification depth (2/3 of 27 block producers + 1). Payouts already wait for solidified blocks.
- **Config.** `PLATFORM_TRX_ADDRESS` (optional; Tron payouts wait without it) and `TRX_GAS_WALLET_KEY` (optional, sealed in SOL's form). `npx tsx src/scripts/create-tron-gas-wallet.ts` makes the key (purpose `tron-gas-wallet-key`). ⚠️ the script was not run.

Testnet and stagenet are refused. ✅ examples from https://github.com/monero-project/monero `tests/functional_tests/validate_address.py`.
  - ⚠️ **For owner review: integrated addresses are refused** as payout and refund addresses, and as `PLATFORM_XMR_ADDRESS`. `monero-wallet-rpc` v0.18.5.4 can't build a transaction to an integrated address with a second destination ("transaction was not constructed"), and every payout above 0% has a platform output. - **Payments (`getPayments`).** `get_address_index` maps the deposit address to its index; an address not in the wallet answers `undefined`, never `[]`. Then `get_transfers` (`in` and `pool`, `subaddr_indices: [index]`). ✅ regtest.
  - One entry per txid, with the amount that subaddress received in it. A transaction that pays two invoices counts for each with its own share.
  - Confirmations come from the wallet (0 in the pool). `seenAt` is the wallet's timestamp: first seen for the pool, block time once mined.
  - Left out: a pool transaction with `double_spend_seen`, and any transfer with a non-zero `unlock_time` (it can't be spent; it stays in the wallet for a person).
  - ⚠️ The wallet's view lags the chain by its refresh (every 20 s by default for `monero-wallet-rpc`); not measured on mainnet.
- **Confirmations.** `required_confirmations` stays 1 (kept). ⚠️ The spec proposes 10 (owner approval pending). Separately, an output can be spent only 10 blocks after its block, so a payout waits until then anyway ("waiting N blocks for the deposit's outputs to unlock"). ✅ regtest.
- **Payout, in order.** ✅ regtest unless marked.
  1. The destination must be a mainnet standard address or subaddress. It must not be the deposit, the platform fee address, or any address of the platform wallet itself (`get_address_index` succeeds). Otherwise the payout is `failed` with a where-the-money-is message naming the subaddress index and deposit address, and nothing is sent.
  2. The open wallet must hold the deposit address at that index (`get_address`). Otherwise `pending` with a review note ("is the right wallet open?"). ⚠️ not tested.
  3. While some of the subaddress's outputs will unlock within the normal 10 blocks, it waits. Outputs locked longer (by a payer's own unlock time) are left behind. ⚠️ The long-lock case is not tested: the wallet RPC can't make one.
  4. Received = the subaddress's unlocked outputs (`incoming_transfers`, available). This must equal its unlocked balance read just before.
  5. F1: platform fee = ceil(received × rate). One `transfer` call:
     - `subaddr_indices: [index]`, so only this invoice's outputs are spent;
     - destinations: the destination gets received − platform fee, the platform gets the platform fee;
     - `subtract_fee_from_outputs: [0]`: the network fee comes off the destination's output;
     - priority 1, `do_not_relay`, `get_tx_key`, `get_tx_metadata`.
     At 0% the platform output is left out (the wallet refuses a zero amount, error -46). There is no change output, because the destinations add up to exactly the inputs.
  6. Before saving, the build is checked. Its `spent_key_images` must be exactly this subaddress's outputs, and its per-destination amounts (`amounts_by_dest`) must equal F1 with its own fee as the network cost. Otherwise `pending` with a note, and nothing is sent. ✅ Mutation check: without `subaddr_indices`, 7 tests turn red; also without the key-image check, 5.
  7. The checkpoint (`tx.raw`) is `{v: 1, txid, txKey, metadata}`. It is saved, then `relay_tx` sends it.
- **Proof.** For each planned receiver, `check_tx_key` with the transaction's secret key reads from the chain the exact amount that address received. Paid at 1 confirmation. A destination that received a different amount fails with a message (txid, and how to check it); a platform difference adds a note. ✅ regtest: exact amounts, also read from the receivers' own wallets.
- **Crash safety.** ✅ regtest.
  - Nothing is relayed before the save returns true. A false save relays nothing.
  - With a checkpoint, `check_tx_key` comes first. If the daemon does not know the transaction, the same `metadata` is relayed again. `relay_tx` is idempotent, also after mining.
  - A new transaction is never built while a checkpoint exists: a rebuild would spend the same outputs.
  - If relaying fails ("Failed to commit tx", for example because something else spent the outputs), the payout stays `pending` with a review note.
  - Shown: after a crash right after the save, the re-run sends the saved transaction once, and the wallet's history shows one outgoing transfer. A call after `paid` returns the same proof.
  - Mutation check: relaying before the save turns 2 tests red.
- **Isolation.** Two invoices funded in one transaction: paying one leaves the other's outputs and balance untouched. ✅ regtest.
- **Too small to forward.** If the wallet answers -16 ("tx not possible") or -17 (not enough money) to the build, or F1 leaves nothing: `too_small`, and nothing is saved or sent. ✅ regtest (1,000,000 piconero).
- **Network cost estimate (payer-pays).** Weight 1,562 × the daemon's `get_fee_estimate` `fees[0]` (priority 1), rounded up to `quantization_mask`. ✅ measured on v0.18.5.4 regtest.

  - ⚠️ A hard fork that changes the transaction format (FCMP++) changes the weight: re-measure then.
  - `monero-wallet-rpc` has no fee estimate call, so the estimate needs a monerod: optional `MONERO_DAEMON_RPC_URL` (⚠️ new setting). Without it, XMR payer-pays answers `409 PAYER_PAYS_NOT_SUPPORTED`. ✅ `tests/routes/xmr-invoices.test.ts`, `tests/invoice/network-cost.test.ts`.

### Running XMR in production (owner)

What the platform must run before XMR goes live. ⚠️ None of this was run outside regtest.
- **A monerod.** The wallet needs a synced node: your own (a pruned node, `--prune-blockchain`, is enough) or a trusted remote node. A remote node learns which outputs the wallet asks about; your own keeps that private. The same node, run with `--restricted-rpc`, can serve `MONERO_DAEMON_RPC_URL`.
- **monero-wallet-rpc with a hot wallet.** For example (⚠️ not run):
  `monero-wallet-rpc --daemon-address 127.0.0.1:18081 --wallet-file /var/lib/xmr/platform --password-file /var/lib/xmr/platform.pass --rpc-bind-ip 10.0.0.5 --rpc-bind-port 18083 --rpc-login merchant:<long random password> --confirm-external-bind --non-interactive`
  - Then set `MONERO_WALLET_RPC_URL=http://10.0.0.5:18083` and `MONERO_WALLET_RPC_LOGIN=merchant:<the same password>` on the backend.
  - Run the same Monero release for monerod and the wallet. `--allow-mismatched-daemon-version` is a regtest need, not a mainnet one.
- **Who can reach the RPC.** Anyone who can reach it and log in can send every invoice's XMR anywhere.
  - Bind it to localhost or a private interface, and firewall it to the backend's address only.
  - Keep `--rpc-login` on. Digest auth is not encryption: on a network you don't trust, put TLS in front (`--rpc-ssl`, or a tunnel). ⚠️ `--rpc-ssl` was not tried.
  - Never expose it to the internet.
- **Backups.**
  - The 25-word seed restores the keys (`query_key` with `key_type: "mnemonic"`, or `seed` in monero-wallet-cli). Keep it offline.
  - A restored wallet scans only the subaddresses it has created plus a lookahead. So also back up the wallet files (`platform`, `platform.keys`) regularly, or restore with `--subaddress-lookahead 0:<highest subaddress_index among the invoices + 200>`. Every invoice's index is in the database (`subaddress_index`). ⚠️ not tested.
- **Custody.** All XMR passes through, and waits in, one platform wallet until payout. The spec's caveat about calling the service "non-custodial" applies most strongly here. Late payments after the 7-day watch, time-locked payments, and the funds of a failed payout all stay in this wallet until a person moves them.
- **The checkpoint holds the payout's secret tx key and its signed transaction** (`tx.raw`, not selected by default). Neither can spend anything, but the tx key proves (and so reveals) what that transaction paid to whom.

## Swap mode (NEAR Intents)

Ticket 15. Code: `src/swap/swap-port.ts` (the port), `src/swap/near.ts` (the NEAR 1Click adapter), `src/swap/registry.ts` (the provider in use), `src/invoice/swap-payout.ts` (paying into a swap, proving the delivery), `src/invoice/invoice.watcher.ts` (`startSwap`, `followSwap`), `src/invoice/closed-watch.ts` (the fallback swap), `src/routes/invoice/create.ts` (`createSwapInvoice`), `src/routes/pay/index.ts`, `src/invoice/swap-view.ts`, `src/webhooks/webhook-pass.ts`. - **The mode.** `POST /invoices` with `mode: "swap"`, `to_crypto` (the receive coin) and optionally `coins`. Offered: every deposit coin that has a chain adapter and that the swap provider can swap into `to_crypto` (`supports`), plus `to_crypto` itself, which is paid out directly with no swap. Refused: `409 RECEIVE_COIN_NOT_SUPPORTED` (no swap can deliver it, or no chain adapter for it), `409 PAYOUT_ADDRESS_REQUIRED` (no valid payout address for it), `409 NO_COINS_AVAILABLE`, `400` for an unknown coin. The merchant's `swap_max_loss_bps` is copied onto the invoice. ✅
  - The payer chooses at `POST /pay/:id/choose`, as in merchant's-list mode: the rate is locked then (`lockPrice`, see "Pricing"), and `to_crypto`/`to_address` are set from the receive coin and the merchant's current address (`409` if that address is gone). `GET /pay/:id` shows `receiveCrypto` and, per coin, whether it is swapped; never the merchant's address. ✅
  - **XMR** is neither offered nor accepted as a receive coin: NEAR does not list it (no `near_asset_id` in `cryptos.json`). **Ticket 24 plugs in at `src/swap/registry.ts`**: Exolix becomes a second `SwapPort`, and `swapProvider` becomes a port that hands each call to the provider whose `supports(from, to)` is true. The routes and the watcher only use `deps.swap` / `ports.swap`, so XMR then appears with no change there. ✅ the refusals (HTTP); the plug-in point: seen in code.
- **The port** (`SwapPort`): `supports(from, to)` (no network), `quote(request)` → `ok` / `too_small` / `unavailable`, `start({depositAddress, txid})` (a hint only), `status(depositAddress)` → the provider's status, amounts, refund reason and txids, or undefined when the lookup failed. It is in `AppDeps` (`swap`, the routes use only `supports`) and in the watcher's ports. ✅
- **NEAR 1Click adapter.** `POST /v0/quote` with `dry: false`, `depositMode: "SIMPLE"`, `swapType: "EXACT_INPUT"`, `slippageTolerance` = the max loss in basis points, `refundTo` = the invoice's deposit address (`refundType: "ORIGIN_CHAIN"`), `recipient` = the merchant's receive-coin address (`recipientType: "DESTINATION_CHAIN"`), `amount` = exactly what will be sent. ✅ `tests/swap/near-intents.test.ts`. That `slippageTolerance` is basis points: in the recorded dry quote, 50 gave `minAmountOut` = 99.50% of `amountOut` (64,092,507 / 64,414,580). ✅ `tests/fixtures/near/quote-dry-btc-usdceth.json`.
  - A quote is refused (`unavailable`, asked again later) when it has no deposit address, needs a deposit memo, has under 4 h left before its deadline, or quotes another `amountIn` than asked. A `400` "amount is too low … try at least N" is `too_small`. ✅
  - The JWT is `NEAR_INTENTS` (Bearer; omitted when empty). Tests never use the real one: the test guard sets `fake-near-jwt`, and no test reaches the network. ✅ seen in `tests/helpers/fake-env.ts`.
  - Fixtures: `tokens.json`, `quote-dry-btc-usdceth.json` and the two `quote-400-*.json` were recorded 2026-11-18 with no JWT and no funds. `quote-built-live-*.json` and `status-built-*.json` were **built** from the dry reply and the OpenAPI spec (📄 https://1click.chaindefuser.com/docs/v0/openapi.yaml), not recorded. ⚠️ A real non-dry quote, a real status reply and `/v0/deposit/submit` have never been seen.
- **The money rule.** F1 on everything at the deposit, in the coin paid (`splitPayout`): platform fee = ceil(received × the invoice's `fee_rate`), sent to the platform in the coin paid; network cost from the merchant's share; the rest goes to the provider's deposit address as one `swap_deposit` transfer. E.g. 100,000 sat at 5% with a 1,000 cost: 5,000 to the platform, 94,000 into the swap. ✅ `tests/invoice/swap-pass.test.ts`.
  - The amount must be quoted before the deposit address exists, but the chain adapter knows the exact amount only when it builds the payout. So: quote the estimate, let the adapter plan, and refuse its checkpoint save (nothing is sent) if its `swap_deposit` differs from the quote; then quote that amount, up to 3 times. ✅ ("re-quotes when…"). ⚠️ 3 attempts is the previous engineer's default.
  - A swap invoice is quoted at most once every 5 minutes (`swap_quoted_at`), since each quote opens a deposit address. ⚠️ default.
  - **Too small**, nothing sent, `outcome: "too_small_to_forward"`, status stays `confirmed` and it is tried again: when F1 leaves nothing, or when the provider says the amount is below its minimum. ✅
  - Paid in the receive coin itself: a direct payout, no swap. ✅
  - A crash right after the `swap_deposit` checkpoint was saved: the next pass sends that saved transaction once and asks for no new quote. ✅
- **Completed** = the provider reports `SUCCESS` **and** the receive coin's chain adapter shows, at the merchant's address, payments whose txid is one the provider names, each with the coin's required confirmations. Hex txids are compared without case or `0x`; others exactly. `amount_delivered` and `received_amount` = the sum the chain shows (not the provider's figure). Below the quote's `minAmountOut`: completed with a review note. `SUCCESS` with no delivery seen: stays `processing`, review note after 24 h. ✅
  - ⚠️ This needs the receive coin's `getPayments(merchant address)` to list the delivery. A merchant address with a long history (BTC/LTC: over 50 Blockbook pages) answers undefined, and the invoice then stays `processing` with the 24 h note until a person checks. Not tested on a real chain.
- **The swap record** (`swap` on the invoice, and on a swap refund route): `provider`, `deposit_address`, `recipient`, `receive_crypto`, `status` (the provider's: `PENDING_DEPOSIT`, `KNOWN_DEPOSIT_TX`, `INCOMPLETE_DEPOSIT`, `PROCESSING`, `SUCCESS`, `REFUNDED`, `FAILED`), `deadline`, `amount_in`, `min_amount_out`, `amount_delivered`, `amount_refunded`, `refund_reason`, `destination_txids`, `version`/`notified_version` (webhooks), `quote_response` (the signed quote, not selected by default). ✅
- **Refunded or failed swap.** `REFUNDED`: the invoice becomes `failed` with `watch_until` = now + 7 days, and records `amount_refunded` and the reason. The coins come back to the deposit address (the `refundTo`), the 7-day watch sees them as new money, and refund routing forwards them: refund address → payout address (both for the coin paid) → a fallback swap to the receive coin → held. `FAILED`: also `failed` with the watch, and a note to ask the provider. `INCOMPLETE_DEPOSIT`: a review note. ✅ (`FAILED` and `INCOMPLETE_DEPOSIT`: seen in code only.)
  - The refunded money pays F1 again (📄 spec: refunded swaps go to the refund address, "Fees still apply to this money"). E.g. 92,000 sat back: 4,600 fee, 1,000 cost, 86,400 forwarded. ✅
  - **The fallback swap** is a refund route with `source: "swap_to_receive_coin"`: quoted and paid like the merchant payout's swap (same max loss, refund to the deposit address), states `processing` → `swapping` (the deposit reached the provider) → `paid` (delivery seen), `refunded` or `failed`. ✅ succeeds; ✅ refunded, then held, with no third swap; ✅ held money is forwarded without a swap once the merchant sets a refund address.
  - **No loop:** once any fallback swap of the invoice came back (`refunded` or `failed`), refund routing never swaps again for that invoice: the money is held for manual handling. A fallback swap that `FAILED` holds at once and ends the watch. ⚠️ "any fallback swap of the invoice" (not only this money's) is the previous engineer's stricter default.
  - So the invoice never ends `failed` without a watch because of a swap: both swap failures set `watch_until`. ✅ (`REFUNDED`).
- **Timing defaults** (⚠️ all the previous engineer's): the quote deadline asked of NEAR is now + 24 h; a quote with under 4 h left is refused; quotes at most every 5 min; 3 quote attempts; the stuck-swap note after 24 h, like a payout. The default max loss when neither the invoice nor the merchant has a valid one is 50 bps (📄 spec story 28).
- **Swaps saved before ticket 15.** The legacy code ran BTC (and TON) deposits into a NEAR swap inside the payout, with the platform wallet as NEAR's refund address. Such an invoice (any mode, `crypto` ≠ `to_crypto`) is now handled like this:
  - Still `confirmed` at deploy: it gets a new swap from the new code. ⚠️ seen in code, not tested.
  - `processing` with a saved swap: the new code follows it, completes it when the delivery is seen, and for a refund that went to another address than the deposit says where it went and asks for a hand payment. ✅ ("a swap saved before ticket 15").
  - A BTC one saved on `main` has `tx.raw` but no `transfers[]`: the BTC adapter rebuilds the transfers from the saved hex as `swap_deposit` once it confirms, and the swap is then followed. ⚠️ seen in code, not tested.
  - **Deploy rule:** before deploying, check that no TON invoice is `processing` with `to_crypto` ≠ `ton`. If the new TON adapter ever had to rebuild its saved payout, it would treat the old NEAR deposit address as a TON payout address (⚠️ seen in code, not tested). None should exist, because TON has never run live (see "Facts"). ⚠️ not checked against the production database.
- **Not verified:** no real swap has run (NEAR has no testnet; the owner runs the mainnet swap). NEAR's real status replies, its delivery txid formats per chain, its refund fee (`refundFee` in the quote) and whether its refunds to our deposit addresses arrive as the watch expects are all unseen. ⚠️

## Refund routing and the 7-day watch

Code: `src/invoice/closed-watch.ts` (the watch and routing), `src/invoice/payment-ledger.ts` (which payout carried which payment), `src/invoice/cancel.ts`. Tests: `tests/invoice/refund-routing.test.ts` (37/37, fake chain that sweeps the deposit), `tests/routes/invoice-cancel.test.ts` (10/10), `tests/webhooks/webhook-pass.test.ts` (16/16), `tests/chains/ton-adapter.test.ts` (15/15, emulator), all 2026-11-18.

- **The watch.** When an invoice closes, it stores `watch_until` = the close time + 7 days. The close sites: expiry (every mode), the two cancels, the merchant payout completing, and a swap completing, refunded or failed (`followSwap` in `src/invoice/invoice.watcher.ts`). ✅ `tests/invoice/swap-pass.test.ts` (19/19), 2026-11-18, for the swap sites.
  - A closed invoice (`expired`, `cancelled`, `completed`) is looked at from the pass after it closed, at most every 5 minutes; a refund route in flight is followed every pass. ⚠️ The 5 minutes is my default: each look is one chain lookup, so the load grows with a week of closed invoices. ✅ the timing itself.
  - Each look records the chain's payments in `payments[]`. A payment first seen after `watch_until` is not recorded.
  - `failed` invoices are watched only when the failure set `watch_until`: a swap that was refunded or failed (its coins come back to the deposit). Any other `failed` invoice is not watched: a person handles that deposit. ✅ `tests/invoice/swap-pass.test.ts` ("fails the invoice but watches its deposit for 7 days"), 2026-11-18.
  - Invoices closed before this was deployed have `watch_until: null` and are never watched. ✅
- **What routes.** Every payment no payout has carried yet, once it has the chain's `requiredConfirmations`, whatever the reason it is there:
  - partial money at expiry (outcome `partially_paid`, which stays);
  - money after a cancel;
  - money after completion;
  - late money after expiry.
  ✅ one table row each.
- **Where it goes.** The merchant's refund address for the deposit asset; else their payout address for it; else nothing is sent and the outcome is `held_for_manual_handling`, with a `review_reason` naming the amount, the payments and the deposit address. An address the adapter's `isValidAddress` refuses counts as missing. Addresses are read at routing time: if the merchant sets one while the watch runs, held money is forwarded. ✅
  - Since ticket 15 a third step comes before "held": swap it into the invoice's receive coin (`to_crypto`), when that is another coin, the swap provider and a chain adapter support it, and the merchant has a valid payout address for it. Not after any fallback swap of this invoice came back (refunded or failed): then the money is held, so there is no loop. See "Swap mode (NEAR Intents)". ✅ `tests/invoice/swap-pass.test.ts` (19/19), 2026-11-18.
- **Fees.** F1 at the invoice's `fee_rate`, through the adapter, like a merchant payout (`destinationKind: "refund_route"`). The table checks exact amounts, e.g. 50,000 sat at 5% with a 1,000 network cost gives 46,500 to the refund address and 2,500 to the platform. ✅
- **Too small to forward.** If `splitPayout` with the adapter's `estimateNetworkCost` leaves nothing, the adapter is not called. If the estimate is unavailable, the adapter is asked. If the adapter answers `too_small` (its dust or rent limit), nothing is sent either. Both set `outcome: "too_small_to_forward"`, and the money stays at the deposit. A top-up inside the watch is forwarded together with it, and the outcome goes back. At the watch's end it stays `too_small_to_forward`. ✅
- **One route at a time, each with its own checkpoint.** `refund_routes[]` holds one element per route, numbered from 1: `destination`, `source` (`refund_address`, `payout_address`, `swap_to_receive_coin`), `state` (`processing`, `paid`, `failed`; a swap route also `swapping` and `refunded`), `swap` (a swap route's record), `raw` (its checkpoint, not selected by default), `swept`, fees, times, and `notified` for the webhook.
  - The first checkpoint save pushes the element. That push is compare-and-set on the route number and on `watch_version`, so of two overlapping runs only one saves.
  - Later saves are compare-and-set on the route's own `raw`.
  - A route never sees the merchant payout's `tx.raw` or an earlier route's checkpoint.
  - Its planned transfers go to `transfers[]` with `payout: n`.
  ✅ the crash test (killed after the save: the next pass hands the saved checkpoint back, and only one broadcast happens), the race test, and "routes a second late payment in its own route".
- **A failed route** keeps the adapter's where-the-money-is message in `review_reason`, marks its planned transfers `failed`, sets `held_for_manual_handling`, and ends the watch (`watch_done`). The status does not change. ✅
- **A route pending 24 hours** after its save gets a review note, as a merchant payout does. ✅
- **`minimumAmount: 1` for refund routes**: forward whatever the deposit holds. The merchant payout's minimum minus 75 nanoton would never be met by TON money held for days. ✅ the route asks for 1 (fake chain with a storage fee).
  - On the emulator, a second payout from a TON deposit that already paid the merchant works: it reached the refund address with the exact F1 amount, non-bounceable, and the merchant was not paid again. ✅ `tests/chains/ton-adapter.test.ts`.
  - USDT on TON: the same, with a top-up only for the TON the deposit is missing. ✅ `tests/chains/ton-jetton/usdtton-adapter.test.ts`.
  - USDT on Tron: the same, with a top-up only for the TRX the deposit is missing (no second activation). ✅ `tests/chains/tron/usdttrx-adapter.test.ts`, 2026-11-18.
  - XMR: a second payout from the same subaddress spends only the outputs that arrived after the first. ✅ `tests/chains/monero/xmr-payouts.test.ts`, 2026-11-18.
  - There the deposit held about 36,400 nanoton less than the 1 TON credited. Once the deposit wallet is deployed (after its first payout), receiving runs its code and the compute fee comes out of the balance. ✅ same test: refund amount 944,965,419 nanoton.
- **What each payout carried** (`payments[].payout`: 0 = the merchant payout, n = route n; absent = still at the deposit). A payout sweeps the whole deposit, so it carried whatever was there, not a list the watcher chose.
  - After a payout is proven, the watcher gives it the payments in chain order (most confirmations, then first seen) while its swept amount (destination + platform fee + network cost, from the saved plan) is not used up. A payment that only partly fits still counts, because a storage or compute fee can take a little of it. An unconfirmed payment can't have been swept.
  - So late money that reached the deposit before the merchant payout is recorded as carried by it (to the merchant's payout address), and is never routed again. ✅ "records late money that the merchant payout swept…", including a 3-unit storage shortfall.
  - ⚠️ Not shown: BTC payments confirming out of their first-seen order. The ordering by confirmations is meant to cover it.
- **When the watch ends.** After `watch_until`, payments first seen before it can still be routed for 24 hours (`ROUTE_GRACE_MS`), e.g. waiting for confirmations. A route already saved is followed until it lands or fails. Then `watch_done` is set. Anything no payout carried becomes `held_for_manual_handling` with a review note, except money that is `too_small_to_forward`. ✅
- **Money that arrives after `watch_until`** is not recorded, routed or announced. It stays at the deposit address, whose sealed key stays in the database (story 78), so a person can move it with the key. For XMR it stays in the platform wallet at the invoice's subaddress index. There is no tool for that yet (see "Before TON goes live" in requirements). ✅ not recorded (table rows "after the 7 days"); ⚠️ no recovery tool.
- ⚠️ **TON late money after signing.** A TON payout's second message sends everything left to the platform (mode 128). Money that lands between signing and landing goes to the platform wallet, and the adapter's `Late payment:` note says so. The watcher then sees that payment as not carried and tries a route, which waits on an empty deposit. When the watch ends, the payment is held with a "should still be at deposit address" note. The earlier `Late payment:` note is kept beside it, and it says where the money really is. Seen in code; not tested.
- **Deploy rule.** Invoices still open at deploy have no stored first-seen times (see "First-seen times"): deploy while none is `underpaid` or `confirming`. Invoices closed before the deploy are never watched (`watch_until: null`), so money reaching them later is not recorded. Invoices closed after the deploy that were created before it have no `watch_version`; the watch treats a missing one as 0. ✅ the test invoices are inserted without it.
